FIELD4 hardware architecture proposal
12 October 2026
Design proposal. Verify hardware and unresolved items before power.


Pi 4 + Pico: the practical first robot build
Reusable sensing / navigation contracts, four independent drive channels, and a hardwired stop.
Recommendation: use the Pi 4 for camera, lidar, IMU, mapping, perception and planning. Connect a Pico over USB for four closed-loop motor channels, encoder counting and local timeout handling. Leave the Zero W out of the first build.
Four FIT0185 motors and four Pololu #4035 carriers. Use one carrier for each wheel.
Encoder connector and signal levels; exact Pico/IMU/lidar adapter variants; physical E-stop hardware; fuse/wire ratings; current-limit verification and regenerative energy protection.
The proposed 10 kΩ VREF resistor gives about 1.46 A peak chopping per motor at 3.3 V SLEEP. That is a test starting point, not proof of sufficient turning torque. The carrier is rated about 2.1 A continuous in the vendor’s test conditions, below the motor’s unrestricted 7 A stall current. [S1-S4]
Start tethered at 12.0 V with the 5 A bench supply and a separate Pi supply. A battery is still unselected. A 14.6 V fully charged pack cannot be assumed safe for 12 V motors just because the drivers accept it.


Wiring decisions and connector discipline
Use the diagrams as functional net maps. Final harness and component selections are still required.

Pi 4 sensor connections
B0309 connects to the Pi 4 camera CSI port using the matching 15-to-15-pin ribbon. Power off before inserting it; verify cable contact orientation at both ends. Lens focus range is specified as 1.5 m to infinity, so near-field image quality needs testing. [S9]

WT901: UART at a verified safe level
Proposed header: Pi pin 1 (3.3 V) to VCC; pin 6 to GND; WT901 TX to pin 10 / GPIO15; WT901 RX to pin 8 / GPIO14. Match the actual TTL module, not a similarly named RS-232/485 unit. With motors disconnected, verify supply and TX amplitude before connecting the Pi. [S8,S10]

Serial configuration
Use an enabled hardware UART with no Linux console on those pins. Verify the actual /dev/serial0 target and Bluetooth interaction; PL011 is preferable to an unfixed mini-UART clock. Discover the IMU’s stored baud rate first, then validate the chosen output rate and checksum. [S8]

Lidar: keep the vendor adapter initially
Connect the A1M8 with its matching factory cable and USB adapter. Verify the sensor and adapter revisions. Budget scanner startup as well as rotation power; measure the complete USB load. Pi 4 permits 1.2 A total across USB ports. A powered hub, if needed, must not backfeed the Pi. [S7,S11,S12]

One driver per motor
Use the carrier VIN input, OUT1/OUT2 motor pair, and GND. Ground PMODE for PH/EN. Ground IMODE intentionally for fixed-off-time current chopping. Do not leave PMODE floating. Add a nominal 10 kΩ from VREF to GND on each carrier; keep the existing CS network intact. [S2-S4]

Default-off signal chain
3.3 V AND output is the only common SLEEP driver. Give ARM and SLEEP external 10 kΩ pulldowns; add defined fail-low RUN/WD signals and local 100 nF bypass. A watchdog trip must latch permission OFF, not periodically retry. Never feed VREF from an independent live source through the carrier’s SLEEP link. [S3,S14]

Encoder level protection
Supply encoder VCC with regulated 5 V. Buffer eight A/B lines through a 3.3 V SN74LVC541A; define both active-low OE inputs and decouple at VCC. Verify output topology before adding pull-ups. Keep motor and encoder pairs distinct; no wire colours or unverified connector cavity numbers are prescribed. [S1,S13]

Feedback without defeating protection
Join open-drain FAULT outputs with a pull-up to 3.3 V and retain individual test points. External ADC inputs require high-impedance, protected CS conditioning; loading CS changes current limiting. Do not add a large filter capacitor directly to CS. Native Pico ADC pins are not 5 V tolerant. [S4-S6]


Control loops, messages and the simulator boundary
Every timing below is a proposed engineering target to measure, not a Pi benchmark or safety guarantee.

Pi: observation and local navigation
Acquisition timestamps, raw sensor logs, validity and calibration IDs feed one estimator/navigation boundary. Start with 100 Hz encoder/IMU handling, 20-50 Hz local planning, and a 50 Hz motor target stream. Camera inference begins around the simulator’s 3 Hz / 640×360 budget only after an ARM-compatible runtime is demonstrated.

Pico: bounded motor execution
Use a compiled Pico SDK implementation with hardware PWM and PIO or verified interrupt encoder counting. Initial targets: 20 kHz PWM and 200 Hz wheel-speed control. Use measured elapsed time, anti-windup, acceleration limits and a zero-speed transition before reversing. Keep encoder snapshots and safety checks independent of USB parsing delays.

Counts, geometry and wheel targets
Record FL/FR/RL/RR polarity so positive means robot-forward. DFRobot’s 2096 CPR label does not establish the x1/x2/x4 software convention; measure counts per output revolution. Use measured effective radii and skid-steer track, not only CAD’s 48 mm radius / 229 mm track. Initial v±ωb/2 side targets need floor-slip calibration. [S1,S17]

Timestamp discipline
Maintain a Pi-to-Pico monotonic clock mapping with offset/uncertainty checks. Timestamp lidar beams or model sweep timing; a complete rotating scan is not instantaneous. Log data age, sequence gaps and dropped packets. Unknown space and missing data must remain distinct from clear space.

Command contract over USB CDC
Use framed binary packets (for example COBS + CRC) with protocol version, length, session/boot ID, mission epoch, monotonic sequence, four bounded wheel targets, source-observation ID and expiry. Reject malformed, repeated, out-of-session and expired commands. Accept the newest valid command only; do not queue motion.

Lease and stop semantics
Propose a maximum 100 ms motor lease. Convert sender expiry through the validated clock map; also impose a receive-age ceiling, so a delayed packet cannot renew old motion. Expiry, STOP or fault clears all targets and latches disarmed. Define and test normal brake-then-coast behavior separately from immediate E-stop/coast.

No automatic return to motion
BOOT → DISARMED → explicit ARM with zero targets → RUN. Any E-stop/watchdog fault forces latched STOP. Clear old commands, check hardware RUN feedback, require deliberate physical reset plus a fresh software session/arm. Watchdog service happens only after successful control-loop checks and output updates.

What v07.3 can actually reuse
Python perception is reusable in principle; Linux ARM dependencies and Pi thermals/latency remain unvalidated. Mapping, mission and planner modules are still GDScript, with simulated hardware calls in main.gd. Extract adapters and use replay parity tests before choosing headless Godot versus a port. No drop-in Pi firmware exists in this patch.


Proposed component set
This list describes the technical design basis, not ownership, purchases, or commissioning.

Pi 4; original Pico (variant to confirm); optional Pi Zero W; Arducam B0309; RPLIDAR A1M8; WT901 TTL; current-limited 12 V bench supply and separate Pi supply. Confirm board variants, RAM/cooling, sensor cables/adapters, supply rating and connectors.

Four FIT0185 motors, four Pololu DRV8874 #4035 carriers, four 96 mm 50A Hogback wheels and four Sonic 6 mm hubs. Verify quantities, shaft/clamp fit and mounting requirements on real parts.

Known mechanical reference
Existing FIELD4 recessed-A1M8 v0.2 CAD is the geometry basis. The current physics file labels mass, friction and torque/current values as uncalibrated estimates. Check harness clearance, strain relief, cooling, centre of mass and rigid sensor mounts on the printed assembly.

Battery / portable power
No final design selected. Retain a reviewed bench configuration until motor loads, bus transients and runtime requirements are measured. Battery chemistry, BMS limits, charger, fuse, enclosure and portable regulators are a later matched design decision.

Required before powered motor testing
Latching NC E-stop; DC-rated contactor/relay with manual-reset circuit; properly fused distribution and return wiring; reverse-polarity input protection; load-side bulk/overvoltage energy protection; rated connectors and secure motor mount. Values and exact products remain to be engineered.

Required signal components
1 × 8-channel SN74LVC541A buffer or verified equivalent; 1 × SN74LVC1G11 gate; independent watchdog/POR plus fault latch; 4 × 10 kΩ VREF resistors (proposal); pulldowns/pull-ups and decoupling. Breakout/package pin maps must be checked before assembly. [S13,S14]

Power, sensing and protection details
Regulated protected 5 V encoder rail; bus-voltage divider/protection; a high-impedance 4-channel current-sense ADC front end for per-wheel telemetry; suitable logic rail protection. Reserve Pico GP20/21 for I2C and GP26 for bus voltage. The exact ADC and analogue component values remain open.

Tools and conditional additions
DMM plus oscilloscope with suitably rated probes; logic analyser optional; current measurement; infrared/contact temperature measurement; secure wheel-off-ground fixture. Add a non-backfeeding powered USB hub only if measured startup demand requires it. Bumpers and drop-off/body-height obstacle sensing may be required for floor autonomy.


Staged bench bring-up with explicit go/no-go gates
Progress by observed evidence. Keep people, pets, loose clothing and fragile items away from moving parts.

1  Identify and inspect - everything off
Photograph board labels, connectors and wiring orientation. Confirm continuity, no shorts, motor-versus-encoder pairs and power polarity. Read exact pinouts and match the harness. Verify fuses protect the chosen wire and connectors. Do not use solderless breadboard paths for motor current.

2  Logic and sensors only
Leave motor VIN disconnected. Boot Pi and Pico, enumerate camera/lidar/IMU, and record raw streams. Confirm encoder rail separately and scope A/B through the buffer while turning each shaft safely. Measure the actual counter convention, polarity, stopped noise and missed/invalid transitions.

3  Prove default-off hardware
With outputs observed and no motors attached, cycle USB, Pi, Pico and motor supplies in different orders. Exercise boot, reset, unplugged USB, malformed/replayed packets, frozen control loop, E-stop wire break and brownout. No case may cause an unintended SLEEP high or automatic re-arm.

4  One motor, securely restrained
Set the supply to 12.0 V with a conservative current ceiling, initially one wheel off the ground. Verify direction, encoder signs, speed loop and the actual hardware trip threshold. Inspect motor/driver temperature. Stop if current limit, supply foldback, noise or oscillation makes behavior unclear.

5  Capture energy and stopping behavior
Scope the motor bus during low-speed deceleration, reversal, lease expiry and E-stop. Confirm the independent clamp protects with the source disconnected. Measure brake and coast response separately. Do not hand-stall a shaft or hold a motor against its unrestricted stall current.

6  Four channels on the stand
Repeat current/thermal checks for every carrier. Exercise all wheels and USB/sensor startup together while staying within the 5 A supply envelope. Verify that noise does not create false counts, resets, invalid IMU packets or undervoltage. Record the summed input current separately from winding current.

7  Slow, supervised floor test
Proceed only after hardware stop, fuse/energy protection and stop distances pass. Begin at approximately 0.05 m/s in a clear, level, bounded area with E-stop in hand. Test straight driving and gradual turns before pivoting; skid-steer turning can demand much more current. No stairs or unattended people-following.

8  Transfer evidence back into software
Replay recorded sensors against the same estimator/planner. Calibrate wheel radii/track, camera/lidar mounts, delay, IMU bias and drive limits. Benchmark sustained perception under thermal load. Re-run Stop, stale-data and recovery regressions, then assess extra obstacle/drop-off sensors before broader autonomy.


Power sizing and stopping: remaining engineering work
The current packet proposes the architecture; it deliberately does not guess a battery, fuse, wire gauge or relay rating.

The 5 A supply is a staging tool
Four unrestricted 7 A stalls imply 28 A, which the bench supply cannot provide. Four proposed 1.46 A winding-current limits sum to roughly 5.8 A; supply current differs with PWM and can exceed 5 A at high duty. Supply foldback is not a substitute for per-wheel current limits or a validated stop.

12 V versus 14.6 V
A 14.6 V pack is 21.7% above the motor nominal voltage. A 12/14.6 ≈ 82% PWM ceiling is only a first-order average-voltage idea; it does not prove safe startup, heating, transient or fault behavior. Do not connect such a pack directly under this proposal.

Portable rail decision
Either select a motor/source pairing with confirmed voltage compatibility, or engineer a regulated motor rail for the battery’s complete range and regenerative behavior. A normal step-down regulator may lose regulation near low battery voltage and may be unable to absorb braking energy. Battery charging is a separate controlled system.

Protection is an energy calculation
Fuse choice depends on load profiles, fault current, wire/connector ampacity and breaking capacity. Put bypass/bulk at each driver after its fuse. Size local and common energy sinks for braking and an opened branch fuse that isolates a driver. Capacitor headroom is ½C(Vmax² - Vinitial²); it is finite. [S15,S16]

Source cut does not remove all energy
Motor inductance, rotating wheels and bus capacitors remain after K1 opens. Keep protective energy absorption on the driver side of K1. A full battery, BMS cutoff, reverse-blocking device or non-regenerative supply can remove the intended energy return path. Do not assume a TVS handles repeated braking. [S15,S16]

Fail-low logic is necessary but insufficient
GPIO reset states, input pulls and an independent watchdog reduce failure modes but do not make a safety-rated controller. Watchdog failure must latch OFF. The contactor must be DC-rated and manually reset; its coil suppression affects release time. No MCU GPIO may switch the coil directly. [S14,S18]

Ground and power sequencing
Route high-current motor loops separately from low-level encoder/IMU wiring. Provide a common low-impedance reference at distribution, with individual motor returns. USB supplies add ground paths; inspect bench earth bonding before using grounded test instruments. Do not disconnect protective earth or parallel independent 5 V sources.

What closes the design
Record actual robot/payload mass, sustained and turning current, temperatures, USB load and worst observed bus overshoot. Then select and document fuse curves, connector/wire ratings, contactor/latch/watchdog, regulator, ADC protection and energy sink. Have the completed harness reviewed before floor operation.
